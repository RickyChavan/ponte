# Conversion and Trust Review: Six Fictional Visitors Browse the Ponte Website

Crowdcast simulation `sim_6ad9e1993c16` (creative mode, 6 key agents, 16 rounds), run 2026-10-22.

## Read this first: status of these findings

- **Hypotheses, not evidence.** All six visitors (Rohan Mehta, Sunita Joshi, Karthik Raman, Neha Kulkarni, Arjun Nair, Meera Iyer) are fictional, illustrative personas. Everything they "thought", "said" or "did" was generated by the simulation. None of it is evidence about real visitors. Test every finding with real analytics and real user sessions.
- **No Ponte replies were modelled.** Outcomes like "awaiting reply" are open, not failures.
- **Facts about Ponte come only from `docs/review-seeds/site-snapshot.md`.** Suggestions that add content are conditional on the content being true; Ponte must supply real names, terms and numbers.
- **Some visitor inferences were guesses**, for example reading "bench" as a shared pool, or assuming "Indian results" means India-based people. They are the personas' own inferences, not facts about Ponte.

## Synopsis

Six people open the same single-page site: a commute phone, a slow-4G Android, and four laptops. The first three sections are the best the page does. "You can't run, sell and manage everything alone" lands for Rohan and Sunita. The four pod cards are the strongest element: Rohan reads them as his exact shopping list, Sunita lights up at "sales, accounts, operations", and Meera calls them the first element she would be comfortable quoting. Mean per-round sentiment in rounds 1 to 3 is near neutral (-0.08, 0.00, +0.03).

The slide starts at "Why Ponte" (round 4, mean -0.22) and becomes a drop at About (round 6, mean -0.58, all six negative). About has no names, photos or bios. Karthik calls it "the first real drop-off moment" and Meera notes "a company whose credibility is people, hiding its people". The footer (round 8, mean -0.52) deepens it: no phone number, no registered entity, no social link, and a "Privacy policy" link that goes nowhere. Neha clicks twice and messages her peer "Not a good sign."

The contact form (round 9, mean -0.48) then repels almost everyone. Rohan will not fill eight fields plus consent on a phone. Sunita taps the sticky "Talk to us" bar expecting a call and lands on a form asking for her phone number. Neha cannot read what she would be consenting to. Arjun and Meera read the talent/partner route as a job-application form, not a referral path. In the comparison round (10, mean -0.40) no visitor finds a proof edge over freelancers, agencies, marketplaces, offshore vendors, CA firms or in-house hires.

By round 11 all six have written down the same gaps: who are the people, what does it cost, who owns data and code, what are the exit terms. In round 12 nobody submits the form. **Final tally at round 16: 0 form submissions; 2 direct emails (Rohan day 1, Neha day 3); 1 proxy email (Sunita's son); 1 off-site query (Arjun to a recruiter); 1 LinkedIn credentials request (Meera); 1 bookmark-and-leave (Karthik).** Every visitor who contacted Ponte bypassed the form.

## Simulation parameters

- Mode: creative (role-play of six fictional visitors on a fixed website). 6 key characters, no crowd groups. 16 rounds, one section or decision stage per round; later rounds run into days 3 to 8.
- Sources: site-snapshot.md, visitor-personas.md.
- Every visitor browsed alone; no visitor-to-visitor interaction. Third parties (recruiter, CA, peer, son, friend) appear in narration only.

## Visitor journeys

Sentiment values are per-round values from -1 to 1 from the round logs.

### Rohan Mehta (pre-seed founder, phone, skimmer): emailed hello@ponte.co.in, hedged with a freelancer
- **Journey:** About 30 seconds of attention. +0.1 hero, +0.3 value, +0.4 pod cards (peak). -0.5 at "Costs you can see" (no number), -0.3 at How it works. Skimmed About, scrolled to the footer looking for a shortcut, copied the email. Tapped the sticky bar and bounced at the form (-0.5).
- **Helped:** the "run, sell and manage everything alone" heading; pod cards matching his need; the footer email as a form bypass.
- **Friction:** cost model with no number; form too long for a phone; no signal that the people are not junior; a 2-working-day reply feels slow.
- **Needed answered:** rough monthly cost or how it is calculated; seniority; minimum commitment and exit.
- **Outcome:** three-line email on day 1. By round 14 he began a two-week freelancer trial as a hedge. A reply with a range and proof of seniority might earn a 20-minute call.

### Sunita Joshi (family MSME owner, mid-range Android, slow 4G): no direct contact; her son emailed for her
- **Journey:** -0.3 at the jargon ("Ye pod kya hota hai?"). +0.3 at pod cards. Trust fell at the nameless About (-0.6). Footer scan for digits found only an email (-0.7, her lowest). Tapped the sticky bar expecting a call and got a form asking for her number (-0.6).
- **Helped:** plain lines about running, selling and managing alone; the sales/accounts/operations cards; the four plain steps.
- **Friction:** "pod" and "management as a service"; no phone number; no names, address or photo; English-only copy.
- **Needed answered:** a phone number or named person; what work would be done for a business like hers.
- **Outcome:** forwarded screenshots to her CA and son. The CA said the company "looks new" and to ask for a phone number first. Her son emailed from his own address and number. Her condition: she will phone if there is a number.

### Karthik Raman (CTO, laptop, four tabs open): bookmarked "Revisit when they have clients"
- **Journey:** negative from the hero (-0.2). "A card isn't a team" (-0.4); "NDA and isolation is not IP assignment" (-0.5). About was decisive (-0.8). Wrote six questions in his notes and never sent them. Four-tab comparison in round 10 (-0.8).
- **Friction:** no named engineers, stack, seniority or team count; no IP/code ownership terms; no security practices; no references; no price range; no FAQ; no minimum term or exit. "Bench" seemed to conflict with "one pod, one client" (his inference).
- **Needed answered:** who the engineers are; who owns code and IP; minimum term and exit; security and access control; price range and fee model; one callable reference.
- **Outcome:** dropped Ponte from the shortlist. In an agency demo he realised the agency answers the same six questions "and Ponte's page did not even invite them".

### Neha Kulkarni (COO/CFO, laptop, privacy-first): emailed six written questions on day 3
- **Journey:** noted the slogan as unverified; asked "which lawyer?" at Why Ponte and found no DPDP Act, data location or audit rights ("worse than silence"). Lowest at round 8 (-0.8): the dead Privacy policy link. At the form she could not read what she would consent to (-0.7). Drafted six questions in round 11, held them, sent on day 3 after her peer returned nothing.
- **Friction:** unnamed lawyer; no data location, DPDP or audit-rights statement; no named people or registered entity; a dead privacy link beside a consent checkbox; the order of NDA, contract and data access not shown.
- **Needed answered:** the lawyer's name; data location; DPDP position; audit rights; what happens when a pod member leaves; registered entity and named people.
- **Outcome:** a conditional, delayed conversion: written answers wanted, no call on the horizon.

### Arjun Nair (DevOps engineer, laptop, curious to join): asked a recruiter instead of submitting the talent form
- **Journey:** neutral at the hero, drawn by hours and leave as a headline claim. Stopped dead at "Join our bench" (-0.6: "So I sit idle until a client signs?"). Found no social link in the footer.
- **Helped:** the culture claim (hours, leave, autonomy) is his only hook, though he sees it as copy, not a contract.
- **Friction:** "bench" wording; no pay range or employment model; no founders' names; no remote/hybrid policy; no talent journey in the process section.
- **Needed answered:** who runs Ponte; employee vs contractor; is the bench paid; salary band; remote or hybrid.
- **Outcome:** form unfilled; he will not submit until he has founders' names and a pay band.

### Meera Iyer (incubator programme manager, laptop): replied on LinkedIn asking for credentials
- **Journey:** treated the site as a legitimacy check. +0.2 pod cards ("she could explain it in two sentences"). Flagged "Costs you can see" as a promise she cannot yet pass on. About cooled her tone (-0.5). The talent strip was "the only partner path on the entire page". Compared Ponte with her vetted-partner list, where every other entry has a named contact, a registration and a reference.
- **Friction:** no named leadership on the site even though a named Ponte team member contacted her; no registration details; no client or reference; no referral programme; no phone; dead privacy link.
- **Needed answered:** founders' details; company registration; one reference; referral terms; a phone number.
- **Outcome:** polite hold; will not mention Ponte to her 40-startup cohort until Ponte has a few clients to speak of.

## Questions the site must answer

Each was written down by at least one fictional visitor; none is answered by the current page. Answers must come from Ponte.

- **People and legitimacy (all six):** Who runs Ponte and who are the pod people? Is Ponte a registered entity? Who is the lawyer? Is there any client, reference or track record? Who can I phone?
- **Cost and commitment (Rohan, Karthik, Meera):** Rough monthly cost, or how is the fee built? Fixed fee or percentage? Minimum term and exit?
- **Quality (Rohan, Karthik, Arjun, Neha):** How senior are pod members? Who vets them and who do they report to? What happens if a member leaves? How fast does a pod start?
- **Data, IP and legal (Karthik, Neha):** Who owns code and IP? What security and access controls apply? Where is data stored; is the DPDP Act addressed; are there audit rights? Does "bench" mean a shared pool, and does that conflict with "one pod, one client"?
- **Talent and partners (Arjun, Meera):** Employee or contractor, paid bench, pay band, remote or hybrid? Is there a referral route with terms, separate from the talent form? What happens after the talent form is submitted?
- **Plain-language access (Sunita):** What does "pod" mean, and what work would be done for a business like mine, in Hindi or Marathi if possible?

## Pivotal scenes

- **Round 4, "Costs you can see" with no number.** Rohan, voice note: "Pod expenses plus a fee. So salaries plus a margin. No number." Meera files it as a promise she cannot pass on. Mean sentiment falls from +0.03 to -0.22.
- **Round 6, About.** Sunita: "Beta, ye log kaun hain? Naam bhi nahi likha." Karthik: "No names. No faces. No bios." Meera notes Ponte's own LinkedIn contact has a name and a face while the website does not. All six turn negative; mean -0.58, the lowest of the run.
- **Round 8, the dead privacy link.** Neha clicks twice and messages her peer: "Privacy policy link goes nowhere. Not a good sign." Karthik and Meera click it too. Rohan finds the one useful thing, the email address.
- **Round 12, decisions, none through the form.** Email; forwarded screenshots; bookmark-and-leave; a held draft; a recruiter query; a LinkedIn credentials request. Only Rohan's email is a conversion moment, and it happened because he avoided the form.

## Themes

- **Trust is borrowed, not given.** Sunita relies on her CA and son, Arjun on a recruiter, Meera on a named LinkedIn contact, Neha on her peer. Everyone who held back needed a human voucher the site did not provide.
- **A people-and-proof vacuum.** No names, clients, references, registration or phone. All six wrote down the same four gaps: people, cost, data/code ownership, exit terms.
- **The form is the wrong door.** Eight fields plus consent, a phone field, an unreadable consent line and a call-expecting sticky button pushed visitors to email, proxies and LinkedIn. Zero submissions.
- **Specific words, specific fears.** "Pod", "bench" and "management as a service" read differently by role: confusing to Sunita, a possible shared pool to Karthik and Neha, unpaid waiting to Arjun.
- **Legal claims without evidence read worse than silence.** The dead privacy link confirmed Neha's suspicion.
- **Delayed and indirect conversion.** Only two direct emails; the rest happened off-site, where website analytics cannot see it.

## What the simulation did not model

- Ponte's replies (speed, quality, tone). Rohan's window was still open; Neha's, Sunita's son's and Meera's messages were unanswered when the run stopped.
- Real behaviour data: no traffic mix, bounce rate, scroll depth or conversion rate was used.
- Visitor-to-visitor interaction.
- Layout and device testing: visitors reacted to content, not layout.
- Persona design effect: the seed file states each visitor's fears and thresholds in advance, so the results partly reflect those inputs.

## Methodology note

This narrative was generated by a multi-agent simulation in which six AI characters, each with a persona derived from the seed documents (traits, motivations, fears, decision thresholds), acted over 16 rounds. The result is emergent narrative, not pre-scripted plot. The report draws on the six persona files, the final world state and per-round sentiment from the round logs. Tension values in `report_data.json` are a derived proxy (max(0, -mean sentiment)), not a simulator output.
